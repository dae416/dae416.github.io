Bioinspired Propulsion

Squid-Inspired Nozzles for Enhanced Efficiency and Thrust

Support: DARPA (Young Faculty Award)  ·  NRF Korea (Domestic Postdoctoral Fellowship)
With: Prof. M. Saad Bhamla (Georgia Tech)  ·  Prof. Daeshik Kang (Ajou University)

Squid achieve extraordinary thrust through compliant funnels that passively shape jet vortex formation. We design and characterize squid-inspired flexible nozzles for rotary propulsion systems, conducting PIV and dynamometry experiments to quantify thrust amplification arising from elastic wave-mediated vortex dynamics. Deep-learning Bayesian surrogate models accelerate nozzle geometry optimization across high-dimensional parameter spaces. Earlier work at Ajou University established the fundamental characterization of cephalopod-inspired soft nozzle systems — measuring thrust generation, elastic energy storage, and impulse-release dynamics in compliant structures replicating squid funnel mechanics.

Keywords: bioinspired propulsion, FSI, elastic waves, deep learning, Bayesian optimization, DARPA, soft robotics

Publications: arXiv:2605.03239 arXiv:2605.17319 J. Fluid Mech. 2024 J. Fluid Mech. 2022

Bioinspired Locomotion

Hydrodynamics of Water-Hopping Mudskipper & Biomimetic Robots

Support: NRF Korea (Sejong International Postdoctoral Fellowship)
With: Prof. M. Saad Bhamla (Georgia Tech)

Mudskippers leap across water surfaces using a unique fin-assisted strategy that exploits surface tension, inertia, and fin thrust in concert. We investigate the interfacial hydrodynamics and 3D kinematics of mudskipper water-hopping through high-speed laboratory experiments and biological fieldwork in Borneo. A stereo-camera 3D tracking system enables non-invasive pose estimation of freely-moving animals in natural habitats. Physical and robotic models validate the discovered locomotion principles. Related work covers the Manu jump (how humans create large water-entry splashes through body posture and timing) and Epineuston vortex recapture in tiny water-walking insects, where vortex interactions enhance thrust efficiency.

Keywords: bioinspired robotics, interfacial dynamics, 3D tracking, high-speed imaging, surface tension, water entry, fieldwork (Borneo)

Publications: Integr. Comp. Biol. 2025 J. R. Soc. Interface 2025 bioRxiv (water skaters)

Interface & Multiphase Dynamics

Interface & Multiphase Flow Diagnostics

Support: Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Daewoo Shipbuilding & Marine Engineering, Samsung Advanced Institute of Technology, NRF Korea
With: Prof. Hyungmin Park (Seoul National University)

We developed the first CNN-based optical flow algorithm for bubble velocimetry (DeepBubbleVelocimetry), enabling non-invasive diagnostics at bubble densities up to 58% void fraction — far beyond the ~11% limit of conventional PTV methods. Liquid atomization studies revealed Rayleigh–Taylor instability as the primary atomization mechanism and derived theoretically validated flow regime boundaries. Industrial collaborations addressed spray coating film formation for marine applications (DSME) and superhydrophobic surface effects on bluff-body wakes (SAIT).

Keywords: bubble velocimetry, CNN optical flow, liquid atomization, Rayleigh–Taylor instability, spray coating, superhydrophobicity, PIV

Publications: Sci. Rep. 2022 J. Fluid Mech. 2022 (atomization) GitHub: DeepBubbleVelocimetry

Undergraduate theses supervised — Junwon Seo (SNU, 2020): flow visualization of air jets from bubble rupture  ·  Suhwan Shim (SNU, 2017): superhydrophobic surface effects on sphere wake

Fluid–Structure Interaction & Multi-body Flows

Starting Jets, Sphere Arrays, Membrane Flows & Medical Devices

Support: NRF Korea, Samsung Electronics, Yonsei Severance Hospital
With: Prof. Hyungmin Park (Seoul National University)

A flexible nozzle modifies the vortical structure of a starting jet and augments thrust. Combining hydrodynamic conservation equations with linearized shell theory, we derived and validated the optimal nozzle stiffness for maximum thrust — the foundational result for the squid-inspired propulsion program. Multi-body wake interactions in in-line sphere arrays reveal how downstream spheres reshape upstream wakes, reducing form drag. Electroconvective circulating flows in asymmetric porous membranes were characterized, identifying coulombic force asymmetry as the driver of enhanced ion transport. In collaboration with Yonsei Severance Hospital, we developed medical devices for urinary surgery resulting in three granted patents (2022). Demo video

Keywords: FSI, starting jet, flexible nozzle, sphere array, multi-body wake, electroconvective flow, ion-selective membrane, medical device, patents, PIV

Publications: J. Fluid Mech. 2022 (FSI) J. Membr. Sci. 2021 Phys. Fluids 2018

Undergraduate thesis supervised — Taechang Kim (SNU, 2016): analysis of optimal rowing boat shape using flow visualization
