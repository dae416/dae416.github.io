Bioinspired Propulsion

Squid-Inspired Nozzles for Enhanced Efficiency and Thrust

Support: DARPA (Young Faculty Award)  ·  NRF Korea (Domestic Postdoctoral Fellowship)  ·  Labs: Bhamla Lab, Georgia Tech  ·  Kang Lab, Ajou University

2022 – present

Squid achieve extraordinary thrust through compliant funnels that passively shape jet vortex formation. We design and characterize squid-inspired flexible nozzles for rotary propulsion systems, conducting PIV and dynamometry experiments to quantify thrust amplification arising from elastic wave-mediated vortex dynamics. Deep-learning Bayesian surrogate models accelerate nozzle geometry optimization across high-dimensional parameter spaces. Earlier work at Ajou University established fundamental characterization of cephalopod-inspired soft nozzle systems — measuring thrust generation, elastic energy storage, and impulse-release dynamics in compliant structures replicating squid funnel mechanics.

Keywords: bioinspired propulsion, fluid–structure interaction, elastic waves, deep learning, Bayesian optimization, DARPA, soft robotics

Related publications: arXiv:2605.03239 arXiv:2605.17319 J. Fluid Mech. 2024 J. Fluid Mech. 2022

Bioinspired Locomotion

Hydrodynamics of Water-Hopping Mudskipper & Biomimetic Robots

Support: NRF Korea (Sejong International Postdoctoral Fellowship)  ·  Lab: Bhamla Lab, Georgia Tech

Sep. 2023 – present

Mudskippers leap across water surfaces using a unique fin-assisted locomotion strategy that exploits surface tension, inertia, and fin thrust in concert. We investigate the interfacial hydrodynamics and 3D kinematics of mudskipper water-hopping through high-speed laboratory experiments and biological fieldwork in Borneo. A stereo-camera 3D tracking system enables non-invasive pose estimation of freely-moving animals in natural habitats. Physical and robotic models validate the discovered locomotion principles and translate them into bioinspired robot designs.

Related work on surface-locomoting animals includes the Manu jump — studying how humans create large water-entry splashes through body posture and timing — and vortex recapture mechanisms in tiny water-walking insects (microvelia), where Epineuston vortex interactions enhance thrust efficiency.

Keywords: bioinspired robotics, interfacial dynamics, 3D tracking, high-speed imaging, surface tension, water entry, fieldwork

Related publications: Integr. Comp. Biol. 2025 J. R. Soc. Interface 2025 bioRxiv (water skaters)

Interface & Multiphase Dynamics

Interface & Multiphase Flow Diagnostics

Support: Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Daewoo Shipbuilding & Marine Engineering (DSME), Samsung Advanced Institute of Technology (SAIT), NRF Korea  ·  Lab: MFFV Lab, Seoul National University

2016 – 2022

Understanding mass and momentum transfer through fluidic interfaces is crucial for predicting droplet size/velocity distributions in spray generation, fluidic mixing in bubbly flows, and interfacial instability onset. We developed novel measurement methods — including the first CNN-based optical flow algorithm for bubble velocimetry — enabling super-resolved, non-invasive diagnostics at bubble densities up to 58%, far exceeding prior limits (~11%). Liquid atomization studies revealed Rayleigh–Taylor instability as the primary atomization mechanism and derived theoretically validated regime boundaries. Industrial collaborations addressed spray coating film formation for marine applications (DSME) and superhydrophobic surface effects on cylinder wakes (SAIT).

Keywords: bubble velocimetry, CNN optical flow, DeepBubbleVelocimetry, liquid atomization, Rayleigh–Taylor instability, spray coating, superhydrophobicity, PIV

Related publications: Sci. Rep. 2022 J. Fluid Mech. 2022 (atomization) GitHub: DeepBubbleVelocimetry

Fluid–Structure Interaction & Multi-body Flows

Starting Jets, Sphere Arrays, Membrane Flows & Medical Devices

Support: NRF Korea, Samsung Electronics, Yonsei Severance Hospital  ·  Lab: MFFV Lab, Seoul National University

2016 – 2022

A flexible nozzle modifies the vortical structure of a starting jet and augments thrust. Combining hydrodynamic conservation equations with linearized shell theory, we derived and validated the optimal nozzle stiffness condition for maximum thrust — laying the foundation for the squid-inspired propulsion work. Multi-body wake interactions in in-line sphere arrays reveal how downstream spheres reshape upstream wakes, reducing form drag through weakened vortex strength. Electroconvective circulating flows in asymmetric porous membranes were characterized, revealing coulombic force asymmetry as the driver of enhanced ion transport and reduced resistivity.

In collaboration with Yonsei Severance Hospital, we developed medical devices for urinary surgery: (1) a bladder pressure sensor system to determine safe Foley catheter removal timing, and (2) a liquid-jet device generating artificial hydronephrosis to reduce ureter–sheath friction. Using combined shell theory, the critical pressure for safe hydronephrosis induction was derived and validated. These resulted in three granted patents (2022). Product demo video

Keywords: FSI, starting jet, flexible nozzle, sphere array, multi-body wake, electroconvective flow, ion-selective membrane, medical device, patents, PIV

Related publications: J. Fluid Mech. 2022 (FSI) J. Membr. Sci. 2021 Phys. Fluids 2018
